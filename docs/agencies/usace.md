# Army Corps of Engineers (Civil Works): one-page brief

**Priority:** 14 of 33 (score 3.2). **FY2026 outlays:** $28B (USAspending).
**As of:** 2026-10-27. Public data only.

## Bottom line

The Corps spends almost entirely through construction and dredging
contracts, many of them small business set-asides, and it ramps up sharply
after disasters. That makes it a near copy of the DoD contractor screen at a
smaller scale. Bid rigging among a few regional dredging and construction
firms is the second pattern.

## Exposure

- $28B in outlays and $77B in budgetary resources in FY2026, boosted by
  infrastructure and disaster supplemental money.
- Set-aside fronts, bid rigging and inflated change orders are recurring
  case types for the Army CID and DOJ's Procurement Collusion Strike Force.

## Public data we can use

- **FPDS / USAspending:** awards, set-aside type, number of offers,
  modifications.
- **SAM.gov** entities and exclusions; SBA certification lists.
- **DOJ and Army CID** case releases.

## Detection plays

1. **Front companies:** the DoD set-aside screen (see [dod.md](dod.md))
   applied to Corps districts.
2. **Collusion signals:** few bidders per award, stable win rotation, and
   shared officers among "competitors" in a district.
3. **Change-order growth:** contracts whose value grows far beyond award
   compared with peer projects.

## First prototype

Run the DoD contractor integrity screen on Corps awards and add a
district-level bid rotation view.

## Limits

Bid amounts for losing offers are not in FPDS; collusion tests rely on
number of offers and win patterns.

## Who to take it to

USACE Directorate of Contracting, Army CID Major Procurement Fraud Unit,
DoD OIG, and DOJ's Procurement Collusion Strike Force.
