# National Science Foundation: one-page brief

**Priority:** 16 of 33 (score 3.0). **FY2026 outlays:** $8.1B (USAspending).
**As of:** 2026-10-27. Public data only.

## Bottom line

NSF is a grant maker with excellent public award data. Fraud risks are
duplicate funding of the same research across agencies, SBIR award mills,
and undisclosed conflicts. It is a cheap addition to the cross-agency SBIR
screen and a good place to test text-similarity methods.

## Exposure

- $8.1B in outlays in FY2026.
- GAO found 842 of 10,570 SBIR/STTR awardees (almost 8%) had four or more
  fraud risk indicators and about $445M in possibly duplicative awards
  across agencies (2024).

## Public data we can use

- **NSF Award Search:** every award with abstract, PI and institution.
- **SBIR.gov** award data; **NIH RePORTER** for cross-agency overlap.
- **NSF OIG** reports and investigations.

## Detection plays

1. **Duplicate research:** near-identical abstracts funded by NSF and other
   agencies.
2. **Award mills:** firms or PIs with many awards and little output.
3. **Network links** to excluded parties or settled cases.

## First prototype

Use NSF as the test bed for the cross-agency duplicate-award screen.

## Limits

Some topical overlap is legitimate; leads need program officer review.

## Who to take it to

NSF OIG, NSF SBIR/STTR program office.
