\hypertarget{bottom-line}{%
\section{Bottom line}\label{bottom-line}}

EPA spends mainly through grants: state revolving funds for water, and
the large 2022 Inflation Reduction Act programs. Its biggest such
program, the \$20B Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund, became a political
fight over alleged fraud. EPA terminated the grants, and the full D.C.
Circuit ruled on 2026-08-04 that it likely did so unlawfully. A neutral,
evidence-based screen of EPA grantees and subgrantees would be useful to
either side, but the topic is politically charged and needs careful
framing.

\hypertarget{exposure}{%
\section{Exposure}\label{exposure}}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \$17B in outlays and \$57B in budgetary resources in FY2026, much of
  it multi-year grant money.
\item
  Risks: pass-through subgrants to new nonprofits, unsupported costs,
  and contractors on revolving-fund projects.
\item
  EPA does not report a high-risk improper payment program.
\end{itemize}

\hypertarget{detection-plays}{%
\section{Detection plays}\label{detection-plays}}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{New-nonprofit grantees:} recipients formed shortly before
  award, with little revenue history on Form 990, receiving large sums.
\item
  \textbf{Subgrant chains:} money that passes through several entities
  with shared officers before reaching a project.
\item
  \textbf{Contractor screen} on revolving-fund construction.
\end{enumerate}

\hypertarget{first-prototype}{%
\section{First prototype}\label{first-prototype}}

A grantee profile for EPA's largest recent grant programs joining
USAspending, SAM.gov and Form 990 data, with facts only and no fraud
labels.

\hypertarget{limits}{%
\section{Limits}\label{limits}}

Litigation and politics make ``fraud'' claims sensitive; present
findings as risk indicators. Subaward reporting is incomplete.

\hypertarget{who-to-take-it-to}{%
\section{Who to take it to}\label{who-to-take-it-to}}
