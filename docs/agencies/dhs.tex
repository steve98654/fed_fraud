\hypertarget{bottom-line}{%
\section{Bottom line}\label{bottom-line}}

DHS is the biggest new opportunity outside the original eight, for two
reasons. First, customs duty evasion has become a top DOJ priority: a
joint DOJ and DHS Trade Fraud Task Force started in August 2025, and the
largest customs False Claims Act settlement to date (\$54.4M) came in
December 2025. Evasion through shell importers and false country of
origin is a close cousin of SBA shell-entity fraud. Second, FEMA
disaster aid has a long fraud history and publishes recipient-level data
through OpenFEMA.

\hypertarget{exposure}{%
\section{Exposure}\label{exposure}}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Customs: DOJ announced three Task Force resolutions totaling over
  \$100M on 2025-12-18, including Ceratizit (\$54.4M, Chinese goods
  declared as Taiwanese). CBP reports 1,200 investigations from revenue
  allegations.
\item
  FEMA: Public Assistance reported \$0.3B improper in FY2025 (GAO);
  disaster grant and contractor fraud recur after every major storm.
\item
  DHS's own contracts (CBP, ICE, FEMA) run through FPDS like DoD's.
\end{itemize}

\hypertarget{public-data-we-can-use}{%
\section{Public data we can use}\label{public-data-we-can-use}}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Vessel manifest data:} public by regulation (19 CFR 103.31)
  and resold by commercial vendors; names importers, shippers and goods.
\item
  \textbf{Census trade statistics:} imports by product and country, for
  mirror checks against partner-country exports.
\item
  \textbf{CBP EAPA and penalty notices}, DOJ customs settlements.
\item
  \textbf{OpenFEMA:} Public Assistance projects, Hazard Mitigation
  awards, and housing assistance aggregates.
\item
  \textbf{FPDS / USAspending and SAM.gov} for DHS contractors.
\end{itemize}

\hypertarget{detection-plays}{%
\section{Detection plays}\label{detection-plays}}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Transshipment signals:} product lines where US imports from a
  country far exceed that country's recorded exports to the US, then the
  importers driving the gap.
\item
  \textbf{Shell importers:} new importers of record that share addresses
  or officers with firms under penalty, or that appear right after a new
  tariff.
\item
  \textbf{Disaster contractors:} new or out-of-area firms winning FEMA
  work fast, shared owners across bidders, and payees tied to excluded
  parties.
\end{enumerate}

\hypertarget{first-prototype}{%
\section{First prototype}\label{first-prototype}}

A tariff-evasion lead list for two high-duty product groups (for example
aluminum extrusions and plastic resin), built from mirror trade gaps and
manifest data, with importer risk scores and reasons. Validate against
settled cases.

\hypertarget{limits}{%
\section{Limits}\label{limits}}

Entry-level customs data (declared value, duties paid) is not public,
and good manifest coverage needs a paid vendor. Customs fraud cuts
revenue, not spending, so it sits outside improper payment reporting.

\hypertarget{who-to-take-it-to}{%
\section{Who to take it to}\label{who-to-take-it-to}}

CBP Office of Trade, HSI, the DOJ-DHS Trade Fraud Task Force, FEMA's
Fraud Investigations and Inspections Division, and DHS OIG.
