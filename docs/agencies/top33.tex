\textbf{As of:} 2026-10-27. Public data only. Scores are our judgment
from the sources in each brief; they are a starting point for
discussion, not a measured result. This extends \url{landscape.pdf},
which covers the eight agencies in DOJ's whistleblower program, to the
33 largest agencies.

\hypertarget{bottom-line}{%
\section{Bottom line}\label{bottom-line}}

Budget size and fraud opportunity are not the same list. Treasury and
SSA are the 2nd and 3rd largest agencies but rank 8th and 22nd here,
because taxpayer and beneficiary data are not public. The top of the
list is where large fraud-prone spending meets record-level public data:
\textbf{HHS, DoD and SBA} as before, then \textbf{USDA and DHS}, then
\textbf{DOT and GSA}.

Three new agencies stand out:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{DHS (customs and FEMA)} is the biggest new opportunity. Tariff
  evasion through shell importers and false country of origin is a top
  DOJ priority (Trade Fraud Task Force, August 2025) and looks like SBA
  shell-entity fraud.
\item
  \textbf{DOT} is the fastest technical win: Disadvantaged Business
  Enterprise fronts are the same pattern as SBA 8(a) fronts, and states
  publish DBE directories and bid tabs.
\item
  \textbf{GSA} owns SAM.gov and FPDS, the data spine for every brief. A
  GSA partnership improves the data for all 33.
\end{itemize}

Below the top 15, most agencies are either small spenders or have little
public payee data. Their best use is to join shared screens
(contractors, research awards, grantees) rather than to get their own
projects.

\hypertarget{which-agencies-and-how-we-measured-budget}{%
\section{Which agencies, and how we measured
budget}\label{which-agencies-and-how-we-measured-budget}}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Source:} USAspending.gov agency list
  (\texttt{/api/v2/references/toptier\_agencies/}), pulled 2026-10-27.
\item
  \textbf{Fiscal year:} FY2026, all four quarters (October 2025 to
  September 2026), as reported by agencies to USAspending. Figures may
  be revised.
\item
  \textbf{Measure:} we ranked by \textbf{outlays} (money actually paid
  out). We also record \textbf{total budgetary resources}, which
  USAspending labels \texttt{budget\_authority\_amount}; it includes
  balances carried over from prior years, so it overstates insurance
  funds (FDIC, PBGC) and multi-year programs. Ranking by budgetary
  resources would swap in the Farm Credit System Insurance Corporation,
  Millennium Challenge Corporation and CFTC for FCC, PCORTF and NRC; the
  top 25 are nearly the same either way.
\item
  \textbf{What is counted as an agency:} USAspending's ``toptier
  agency'' list, as is. That list includes one trust fund (PCORTF) and
  omits off-budget or non-reporting bodies such as the Postal Service,
  Tennessee Valley Authority and Railroad Retirement Board (about \$5.1B
  net in FY2026 per Treasury's Monthly Treasury Statement), and the
  legislative and judicial branches.
\item
  \textbf{Cross-check:} Treasury's Monthly Treasury Statement (Table 5,
  2026-09-30) gives the same top tier. Totals differ (for example DoD
  \$917B net in MTS versus \$1,345B in USAspending, which also counts
  military trust funds), but the ranking is stable.
\item
  Raw figures: \url{data/top33_fy2026.csv}.
\end{itemize}

\hypertarget{how-we-scored}{%
\section{How we scored}\label{how-we-scored}}

Same method as \url{landscape.pdf}: each agency gets 1 to 5 on four
factors, weighted toward what we can do with public data alone.

Exposure & 30\% & Tens of billions a year in improper payments or fraud-prone spend \\
Public data & 30\% & Record-level data that names payees (providers, contractors, retailers) \\

Score = 0.3 × Exposure + 0.3 × Public data + 0.2 × SBA fit + 0.2 ×
Action. Scores for the original eight are unchanged from
\url{landscape.pdf}.

\hypertarget{recommended-sequence}{%
\section{Recommended sequence}\label{recommended-sequence}}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Now (unchanged): HHS and DoD,} with SBA as the baseline.
\item
  \textbf{Next: DHS customs, USDA, DOT.} DHS and USDA have the strongest
  enforcement pull; DOT reuses the SBA front-company code with new state
  data.
\item
  \textbf{Data partners in parallel: GSA, DOJ, SEC, SSA.} Each makes the
  shared pipeline better: GSA for SAM.gov and FPDS, DOJ for labeled
  cases, SEC for ownership, SSA for death data.
\item
  \textbf{Contained pilots: FCC (E-Rate) and OPM (FEHB exclusions).}
  Small, data-rich, and quick to show results.
\item
  \textbf{Shared screens instead of separate projects} for the rest: one
  contractor screen (DoD, USACE, NASA, NRC, State), one research award
  screen (DOE, NSF, NASA, PCORTF), one grantee screen (EPA, DOJ,
  AmeriCorps, Commerce).
\item
  \textbf{Proposal first, data later: Treasury, SSA, Labor, PBGC.} Big
  money, but the payee data is confidential, so lead with a data-access
  proposal.
\end{enumerate}
