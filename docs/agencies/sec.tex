\hypertarget{bottom-line}{%
\section{Bottom line}\label{bottom-line}}

The SEC spends little, so it is not a fraud target in the usual sense.
Its value here is as a data source and partner: EDGAR filings reveal
ownership, officers and related parties for public companies and many
private offerings, which strengthens the entity spine for every other
brief. The SEC also distributes Fair Fund money to harmed investors,
which can attract fraudulent claims.

\item
  Fair Fund and disgorgement distributions to investors.
\end{itemize}

\section{Public data we can use}\label{public-data-we-can-use}}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist

\item
  \textbf{SEC litigation and administrative releases} naming
  individuals.
\end{itemize}

\hypertarget{detection-plays}{%
\section{Detection plays}\label{detection-plays}}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Entity enrichment:} link federal contractors and grantees to
  EDGAR officers and Form D issuers to find hidden common control.
\item
  \textbf{Bad-actor overlap:} people barred or charged by the SEC who
  also run federal award recipients.
\end{enumerate}

\hypertarget{first-prototype}{%
\section{First prototype}\label{first-prototype}}

An EDGAR enrichment step added to the shared entity pipeline, reporting
award recipients whose officers appear in SEC enforcement actions.

\hypertarget{limits}{%
\section{Limits}\label{limits}}

Most small federal contractors are private and absent from EDGAR except
through Form D.

\section{Who to take it to}\label{who-to-take-it-to}}
