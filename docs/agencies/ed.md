# Education: one-page brief

**Priority:** 6 of 7 (score 2.7). **As of:** 2026-10-25. Public data only.

## Bottom line

Education's measured error rates are low (about 1% for Pell and Direct
Loans), but "ghost student" identity fraud is real and growing. The
department says ID checks saved about $1B. Public data are school-level, so
we can flag the colleges most exposed, not the fake students.

## Exposure

- Pell Grant: 1.07% improper in FY2025 (down from 2.64%). Direct Loan: 0.99%.
  (PaymentAccuracy.gov)
- The department credits mandatory government ID checks with about $1B in
  savings; before them, under 1% of students were verified. Minnesota alone
  reported $12.5M in student aid fraud. (CBS Austin, Apr 2026)

## Public data we can use

- **FSA Data Center:** Title IV volume by school, program reviews, and the
  Heightened Cash Monitoring list.
- **College Scorecard** and **IPEDS** (enrollment, online share, completion).
- **Education OIG reports** and DOJ releases.

## Detection plays

1. **Ghost student exposure:** colleges, mostly online community college
   programs, where aid volume jumps while completion and retention fall.
2. **School risk:** schools on cash monitoring, with sanctions, or with
   owners tied to closed schools.

## First prototype

A school-level ghost student risk ranking, validated against colleges named
in news and OIG cases.

## Limits

Student-level data are protected by privacy law and will not be public.
Real detection needs a pilot with FSA.

## Who to take it to

Federal Student Aid (Enforcement), and Education OIG.
