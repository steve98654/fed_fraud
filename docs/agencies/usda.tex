Public data only.

\hypertarget{bottom-line}{%
\section{Bottom line}\label{bottom-line}}

USDA brings two strong targets: SNAP retailer trafficking and child meal
sponsor fraud, the pattern behind Minnesota's \$250M Feeding Our Future
case. Both are DOJ whistleblower priorities (SNAP and day care). Public
data name the retailers and the grantees, but not the transactions, so
we score entities, not purchases.

\hypertarget{exposure}{%
\section{Exposure}\label{exposure}}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  SNAP: \$10.2B improper (10.9\%). Child and Adult Care Food Program:
  10.5\%. Emergency Conservation Program: 55.5\%. Noninsured Crop
  Disaster Assistance: 10.6\%. (GAO, FY2025)
\item
  Feeding Our Future: \$250M scheme, 78 people charged.
\end{itemize}

\hypertarget{public-data-we-can-use}{%
\section{Public data we can use}\label{public-data-we-can-use}}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{SNAP authorized retailer data} (store type, location,
  authorization dates) and published retailer disqualification actions.
\item
  \textbf{USAspending} grants and payments, including farm program and
  nutrition grants to states and sponsors.
\item
  \textbf{State CACFP sponsor and site lists}, where states publish
  them.
\item
  \textbf{USDA OIG reports} and DOJ releases.
\end{itemize}

\hypertarget{detection-plays}{%
\section{Detection plays}\label{detection-plays}}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Retailer outliers:} small stores in dense clusters, new
  authorizations near already disqualified stores, and owners linked to
  past violators.
\item
  \textbf{Meal sponsor anomalies:} sites claiming more meals than local
  child population supports, sudden growth, and shared sponsors across
  many sites.
\item
  \textbf{Farm disaster payments:} recipients far above local peers, or
  payments tied to entities with shared officers.
\end{enumerate}

\hypertarget{first-prototype}{%
\section{First prototype}\label{first-prototype}}

A SNAP retailer risk map for two states, scoring each store against
nearby peers and known disqualified stores, plus a sponsor screen for
one state that publishes CACFP site lists.

\hypertarget{limits}{%
\section{Limits}\label{limits}}

SNAP redemption data by store are not public, so trafficking is inferred
from location and ownership, not sales. CACFP data vary by state.

\hypertarget{who-to-take-it-to}{%
\section{Who to take it to}\label{who-to-take-it-to}}

The Food and Nutrition Service (Retailer Operations), USDA OIG, and
state SNAP and child nutrition agencies.
