\hypertarget{bottom-line}{%
\section*{Bottom line}\label{bottom-line}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Bottom line}

Most large frauds are invisible inside one program and obvious across
several: the same person drawing unemployment insurance in two states
and a PPP loan that claims them as a full-time employee; a hospice owner
who was debarred by GSA and is still billing Medicare; a dead veteran's
identity cashing SNAP benefits. No agency can see this alone, because
each holds one slice of the identity. This extension builds the shared
method: link people and businesses across program files with
privacy-preserving keys, then run four checks that only a linked view
allows.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Stacking and exclusive programs:} one identity in more
  programs than its profile explains, or in two programs that the law
  says cannot both pay for the same thing at the same time.
\item
  \textbf{Paid after death:} a death recorded in any one program reaches
  every program the identity is paid by.
\item
  \textbf{Paid while excluded:} SAM exclusions, the HHS-OIG LEIE and OPM
  debarments applied to every payment stream, not only the one that
  issued the exclusion.
\item
  \textbf{One identifier, many identities:} one bank account, phone,
  email or device used by people or firms that otherwise do not link
  (roadmap idea X-2).
\end{enumerate}

Phase 1 runs the business side on public data (SAM.gov, USAspending, SBA
loan-level files, CMS enrollment files, exclusion lists). Phase 2 adds
person-level data through Do Not Pay and computer matching agreements
(see the \href{../policy/legal_track.pdf}{policy and legal track}).

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  The roadmap's six cross-agency ideas (X-1 to X-6) all depend on it.
\item
  GAO and the Pandemic Response Accountability Committee found the same
  identities across PPP, EIDL and unemployment insurance; the pandemic
  analytics center that matched them closed when its authority lapsed.
\item
  Treasury's Do Not Pay already matches payments against death and
  exclusion lists, but only for agencies that send it files, and only on
  the identifiers they send. Matching on linked identities catches the
  variants that exact matching misses.
\end{itemize}

\hypertarget{method}{%
\section*{3. Method}\label{method}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{3. Method}

\textbf{Normalize} every name and address the same way: legal suffixes
and punctuation removed, USPS standardization (CASS-certified in
production), phones to E.164, emails lowercased with plus-tags removed.

\textbf{Hash identifiers inside each owner's boundary.} TINs, SSNs and
bank accounts never leave the agency that holds them. Each data owner
computes a keyed hash (HMAC-SHA256) of the normalized value with a key
held by the matching authority, and only hashes are exchanged. Two equal
hashes mean two records share an identifier without revealing it. Keys
rotate per matching agreement.

\textbf{Block, then compare.} Candidate pairs are records that share a
hashed TIN, bank account, phone or email, or a first name token and ZIP.
Blocks larger than 200 records (registered agents, call centers) are
dropped, as hubs are in the network method. Each pair is scored with the
Fellegi-Sunter weights already in the
\href{forensic_methods.pdf}{forensic methods handbook} section 7.

\textbf{Link and review.} Pairs above the upper threshold link
automatically; pairs between the thresholds go to clerical review.
Thresholds are set on 500 hand-labeled pairs to reach 95\% precision, as
the methodology requires.

\textbf{Run the checks} in section 4 on every linked identity.

\hypertarget{privacy-and-safeguards}{%
\section*{5. Privacy and safeguards}\label{privacy-and-safeguards}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{5. Privacy and safeguards}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Legal basis first.} Person-level matching across agencies is a
  computer matching program under the Privacy Act unless an exception
  applies (OIG investigations, Do Not Pay under the Payment Integrity
  Information Act). See the \href{../policy/legal_track.pdf}{policy and
  legal track}.
\item
  \textbf{Minimum necessary.} Only the fields in section 3 cross a
  boundary, and only as hashes where they are identifiers.
\item
  \textbf{Due process.} No payment is stopped on a match alone; the
  Privacy Act requires independent verification and notice before
  adverse action.
\item
  \textbf{Error budget.} Each check reports its false-positive rate from
  the clerical review sample, and checks above 20\% false positives are
  not released.
\item
  \textbf{Bias review.} Match rates by state and by name origin are
  compared, since name-based matching is less accurate for some naming
  conventions.
\end{itemize}
