\hypertarget{bottom-line}{%
\section*{Bottom line}\label{bottom-line}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Bottom line}

The government buys about \$750 billion a year of goods and services
through contracts, and every award is published in FPDS and USAspending.
Three schemes leave traces in those records: \textbf{weak or rigged
competition}, \textbf{contracts that grow after they are won}, and
\textbf{small business set-asides won by firms that are not small or do
not do the work}. This extension turns each into a screen that runs on
public data across every agency, so DoD-1, VA-1, GSA-1 and DOT-1 share
one engine.

\hypertarget{competition}{%
\subsection*{2.1 Competition}\label{competition}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{2.1 Competition}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Single-offer rate} by office and market: competed awards that
  drew one offer. Persistently high rates point to tailored requirements
  or a steered market. Compared within NAICS, since some markets are
  thin.
\item
  \textbf{Incumbent lock-in:} Herfindahl index of vendor shares by
  office and NAICS; high concentration with mostly single-offer
  competitions.
\item
  \textbf{Sealed-bid distribution screens and rotation tests} (handbook
  section 6): low coefficient of variation, high relative distance,
  winners taking turns.
\end{itemize}

\hypertarget{change-order-and-modification-inflation}{%
\subsection*{2.2 Change-order and modification
inflation}\label{change-order-and-modification-inflation}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{2.2 Change-order and modification
inflation}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Growth ratio:} current value / base and all options value at
  award. Robust z-score of its log within NAICS; awards above 3.5 are
  reviewed.
\item
  \textbf{Many modifications just under an approval threshold} (warrant
  limits, cost or pricing data thresholds, legal review levels): the
  modification version of a split purchase.
\item
  \textbf{Out-of-scope growth:} modifications coded as ``additional work
  (new agreement)'' or ``supplemental agreement'' on sole-source
  bridges.
\end{itemize}

Each reason is a question for the contracting officer or SBA, not a

needs the facts behind a shared address.

\hypertarget{thresholds-to-know}{%
\section*{3. Thresholds to know}\label{thresholds-to-know}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{3. Thresholds to know}

Thresholds change with inflation adjustments (FAR 1.109). The October
2025 adjustment raised the micro-purchase threshold to \$15,000 and the
simplified acquisition threshold to \$350,000; check the current FAR
2.101 before relying on these. Bunching tests (handbook section 5.4)
look for excess awards just under each threshold.

\hypertarget{validation}{%
\section*{4. Validation}\label{validation}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{4. Validation}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Positive set: SBA size protests sustained, OHA decisions, DOJ
  set-aside fraud cases, suspension and debarment records with
  procurement causes.
\item
  Release bar as in the review plan: known cases at least five times
  more common in the top 5\% than overall.
\item
  False positives to expect: legitimate scope growth on research and
  development, emergency buys, sole-source awards under statute, joint
  ventures that are allowed to share resources.
\end{itemize}
