# USDA-2: Child meal sponsor capacity screen

**For:** USDA OIG, state child nutrition agencies. **Weeks:** 6-10
(results by 2027-01-22). **Brief:** [USDA](../agencies/usda.md).

## The problem

In the Feeding Our Future scheme, sponsors claimed millions of meals at
sites that served few or no children: a $250M fraud with 78 people
charged. The core signal is simple. A site cannot serve more meals than
there are children nearby, and new sites cannot grow tenfold in a month.

## Data

- State CACFP and summer meal sponsor and site lists with meal counts by
  site (FOIA, Minnesota plus one other state).
- ACS child population by census tract.
- USAspending USDA child nutrition grants; Form 990 filings for nonprofit
  sponsors.

## Detection method

1. **Capacity:** meals claimed per site against children living within a
   reasonable distance.
2. **Growth:** sites and sponsors whose claims jump far faster than peers.
3. **Networks:** sponsors running many sites with shared addresses,
   officers or vendors.

## Validation

Back-test on Feeding Our Future defendants and sites: scored on data from
before the case, they should rank near the top.

## Exposure (public figures, not savings)

- Child and Adult Care Food Program: 10.5% improper in FY2025 (GAO).
- Feeding Our Future: $250M scheme, 78 people charged.
- Savings from this work: **Pending.**

## What we ask for

- **Now:** a USDA OIG contact; help from one state agency to speed the
  site-level meal count request.
- **Phase 2:** sponsor claims and site attendance records, to test claims
  before they are paid.

## What you get, and when

By week 10: a sponsor screen for one state with ranked sponsors and sites,
reasons and a method note other states can run.

## Limits

Site-level meal counts depend on state records requests, which can take
months. Children travel to sites, so capacity is a range, not a hard limit.
