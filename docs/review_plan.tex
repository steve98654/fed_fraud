\hypertarget{bottom-line}{%
\section{Bottom line}\label{bottom-line}}

This plan turns the 33 agency briefs into a dated program of work in two
phases.

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Phase 1 (public data, 2026-11-02 to 2027-03-05, 16 working
  weeks).} Build one shared data spine and case library, then run a
  defined review of every agency using only public data. Seven agencies
  get full prototypes (HHS, DoD, SBA, USDA, DHS, DOT, GSA). Five get
  contained pilots (FCC, OPM, VA, Commerce, PBGC). The rest are covered
  by three shared screens (contractors, research awards, grantees) or,
  where payee data is closed, by an aggregate screen plus a data-access
  proposal. Every agency ends Phase 1 with a written finding and a
  ranked lead list or a reasoned ``nothing actionable from public
  data''.
\item
  \textbf{Phase 2 (internal data, from 2027-03 through 2028).} Use the
  Phase 1 results to win data agreements, starting with Inspectors
  General, who already hold the data and the authority to act on it.
  Internal data moves the work from risky \emph{entities} to specific
  false \emph{claims and payments}, which is what prosecutors need, and
  makes pre-payment prevention possible. The first internal-data pilots
  can realistically start around 2027-07; agreements are the long pole,
  not the analytics.
\end{itemize}

The plan rests on three choices worth checking:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Leads, not accusations.} Public-data findings are risk
  indicators. Nothing is published or named outside a referral to the
  responsible Inspector General or DOJ component.
\item
  \textbf{Build once, apply 33 times.} About 70\% of the Phase 1 effort
  goes into shared assets (entity spine, case library, scoring, lead
  package). Each added agency then costs days, not months.
\item
  \textbf{Start Phase 2 paperwork in Phase 1.} Data agreements take 3 to
  12 months, so access requests go out in week 8, not after Phase 1
  ends.
\end{enumerate}

\hypertarget{scope-and-ground-rules}{%

\hypertarget{what-a-thorough-review-means-here}{%
\subsection{What ``a thorough review'' means
here}\label{what-a-thorough-review-means-here}}

For each of the 33 agencies, the review answers five questions:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Where is the money?} Which programs and payment streams carry
  the agency's outlays, and which are fraud-prone.
\item
  \textbf{What fraud is known?} Improper payment estimates, OIG and GAO
  findings, and charged cases, by scheme.
\item
  \textbf{What can public data see?} Which payees, awards and
  relationships are visible, and which are not.
\item
  \textbf{What does the data show?} Ranked leads from the screens in
  this plan, each with stated reasons and a validation result.
\item
  \textbf{What would internal data add?} The Phase 2 data request,
  access route and expected payoff.
\end{enumerate}

The output for each agency is a \textbf{review memo} (one to three
pages, \texttt{docs/reviews/\textless{}agency\textgreater{}.md}) plus,
where there are findings, a \textbf{lead package} (see ``Lead packages''
below).

\hypertarget{ground-rules}{%
\subsection{Ground rules}\label{ground-rules}}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Public and lawfully obtained data only in Phase 1.} That
  includes federal open data, state public records, court records, FOIA
  releases and licensed commercial data. No scraping behind logins, no
  personal data beyond what the source publishes.
\item
  \textbf{Findings are leads.} A lead needs at least two independent
  risk signals and a written reason before it leaves the analysis team.
  No lead names a person in any public document.
\item
  \textbf{Referral, not publicity.} Leads go to the agency OIG or the
  DOJ component named in each brief. Counsel reviews the referral route
  before the first package goes out, including False Claims Act
  public-disclosure and original-source questions and the DOJ
  whistleblower program at america.gov/fraud.
\item
  \textbf{Reproducible.} Every number in a memo traces to a script and a
  dated data pull in the repo, so results can be rerun and defended.
\item
  \textbf{Scored honestly.} Each screen is tested against known cases
  (see ``Validation'') and the hit rate is reported, including when it
  is poor.
\end{itemize}

\hypertarget{validation}{%
\subsection{Validation}\label{validation}}

Every screen is tested the same way before its leads are used:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Back-test against known cases.} Take entities charged or
  settled in the case library (F5) for that program. Score the
  population as it looked \emph{before} the case became public. Report
  how many known cases land in the top 1\%, 5\% and 10\% of scores.
\item
  \textbf{Hand review a sample.} The investigator reviews 25 top-scored
  entities that are not known cases and records whether a reasonable
  reviewer would want to look further.
\item
  \textbf{Bar for release.} A screen is released when known cases are at
  least five times more common in its top 5\% than in the population,
  and at least half of the hand-reviewed sample is judged worth review.
  Screens that miss the bar are reported as such and reworked or
  dropped.
\end{enumerate}

\hypertarget{lead-packages}{%
\subsection{Lead packages}\label{lead-packages}}

A lead package is what goes to an OIG or DOJ. One package per entity or
network, in a fixed format:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Summary: who, which program, how much money, which scheme it
  resembles.
\item
  Signals: each risk signal with its source record and date.
\item
  Network: related entities, shared addresses, officers, exclusions.
\item
  Comparison: how the entity compares with its peers.
\item
  Known-case match: the charged cases it most resembles.
\item
  Limits: what public data cannot show, and innocent explanations
  checked.
\item
  Provenance: data pulls and script versions used.
\end{itemize}

Packages are reviewed by the investigator and the program lead, then by
counsel for the first ten, before referral.

\hypertarget{phase-2-internal-data-to-identify-and-prosecute-fraud}{%
\section{Phase 2: internal data to identify and prosecute
fraud}\label{phase-2-internal-data-to-identify-and-prosecute-fraud}}

\hypertarget{why-internal-data-matters}{%
\subsection{Why internal data matters}\label{why-internal-data-matters}}

Public data can say \emph{which entities look risky}. It cannot see the
claim, invoice, application or payment where the fraud happens. Internal
data changes three things:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{From entity to transaction.} Score each claim, invoice, loan
  or benefit payment, not just the payee.
\item
  \textbf{From lead to evidence.} Show the specific false statements,
  the dollars paid on them and who received the money. That is what a
  grand jury, a False Claims Act complaint or an administrative action
  needs.
\item
  \textbf{From recovery to prevention.} Run the same checks before money
  goes out: at enrollment, application, award and payment.
\end{enumerate}

\hypertarget{prosecution-support}{%
\subsubsection{Prosecution support}\label{prosecution-support}}

Finding fraud is not enough; cases must hold up. Phase 2 adds:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Evidence-grade pipeline.} Locked data snapshots, logged
  queries, versioned code and reproducible outputs, so an analyst can
  testify to how a result was produced.
\item
  \textbf{Case packages for prosecutors.} For each referral: the scheme,
  the claims or payments involved, total loss, the people who received
  the money, and a list of records to subpoena.
\item
  \textbf{Limitations triage.} Rank cases by time left: five years for
  most federal fraud statutes, ten years for bank fraud and for PPP and
  EIDL fraud, and up to ten years under the False Claims Act.
\item
  \textbf{Recovery focus.} Trace payments to accounts and assets to
  support seizure and forfeiture.
\item
  \textbf{Feedback loop.} Outcomes of referred cases feed back into
  scoring.
\end{itemize}

Two legal limits shape the plan:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Tax data (26 U.S.C. 6103)} can be shared only in narrow cases.
  IRS work should be done inside IRS or TIGTA, with us supplying methods
  and non-tax data, not by moving returns out.
\item
  \textbf{Privacy Act and matching rules} mean cross-agency matching
  needs a matching agreement or an OIG exception. Starting with OIGs
  avoids the longest delays.
\end{itemize}

Recent policy may speed this up: the March 2025 executive order on
eliminating information silos directs agencies to give designated
officials access to unclassified records, and the 2026 anti-fraud task
force makes cross-agency fraud work a stated priority. The precedent to
point to is the Pandemic Response Accountability Committee's Pandemic
Analytics Center of Excellence, which matched data across agencies for
pandemic programs (check its current status: the committee's authority
was set to end in September 2025).

\hypertarget{security-and-governance-requirements}{%
\subsection{Security and governance
requirements}\label{security-and-governance-requirements}}

Agencies will ask for these before sharing data, so they are budgeted
from the start of Phase 2:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  A government-authorized environment (FedRAMP Moderate or High, or
  working inside the agency's or OIG's own enclave).
\item
  An authority to operate for any system that holds agency data.
\item
  Personnel suitability or background checks for analysts.
\item
  Privacy impact assessment, data retention and destruction rules.
\item
  Model documentation: what each score uses, how it was tested, and
  checks for bias against protected groups or places.
\item
  A governance board with the OIG, program office and counsel that
  approves new uses of data.
\item
  A contract vehicle or interagency agreement to pay for the work.
\end{itemize}
